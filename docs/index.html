The problem

Decades of measurements are locked in prose.

Trait databases feed ecological research, from population models to conservation planning. Much of the data behind them exists only as sentences in published papers.

  • Slow to collect

    Someone reads each paper and copies values into a spreadsheet, one at a time.

  • Hard to audit

    Later, nobody can tell which sentence a number came from, or whether its unit was converted.

  • Risky to automate

    General AI tools can invent numbers, and many send your documents to an outside service.

How it works

From PDF to dataset in four steps.

  1. Step 1

    Upload

    Send a PDF to BioParser. You get a job number right away and can check its progress at any time.

  2. Step 2

    Read

    The paper is split into text blocks, each tagged with its page and position. A layout-aware engine handles multi-column articles.

  3. Step 3

    Extract

    A language model on your own GPU finds the measurements. Each one must quote the block it came from, or it is rejected.

  4. Step 4

    Deliver

    Values are converted to grams and millimetres and mapped to the Ecological Trait-data Standard, ready to load into your database.

    Standard mapping in development

Why trust it

Built so every number can be checked.

Evidence on every value

Each value keeps its page, its text block and the exact words it came from. Reviewers can open the source and confirm it.

Your papers stay with you

Papers, models and results stay on your own servers. BioParser uses no outside AI service, so nothing leaves your network.

Ready to grow

Reading, extraction and output are separate parts. New traits, standards and research areas can be added without changing how you submit papers.

What it measures today

Three core traits, in units that line up.

BioParser starts with three basic body measurements. Papers report them in different units, so every value is stored in grams or millimetres and results from different studies compare directly.

Body mass
g or kg → grams
Body length
mm, cm or m → millimetres
Tail length
mm, cm or m → millimetres

Coming later: more traits, and new areas such as diet.
